Our study on acute spinal cord injury (SCI) regulatory networks is published open-access in *Molecular Therapy: Nucleic Acids* ([doi:10.1016/j.omtn.2025.102746](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omtn.2025.102746)).

### Background and Experimental Setup

Acute neurotrauma triggers rapid biochemical cascades that evolve over hours and days. Most genomic studies examine mRNA transcripts or small RNAs in isolation, which obscures post-transcriptional regulation. 

In this work, we profiled matched spinal cord lesion tissue from adult rats across acute post-injury timepoints using three complementary layers:
- **Small RNA sequencing** for microRNA expression.
- **Bulk mRNA sequencing** for gene transcription.
- **LC-MS/MS mass spectrometry** for protein abundance.

### Network Integration & Biological Findings

Rather than treating each dataset as an independent list of differentially expressed features, we constructed an integrated interactome connecting miRNA shifts to target mRNA degradation and corresponding protein depletion.

- **Temporal Dynamics:** The primary regulatory shift occurs within the first 24–72 hours post-injury, characterized by coordinated downregulation of neuroprotective miRNA clusters alongside acute inflammatory signaling.
- **Validation of miR-20a:** Network centrality metrics identified miR-20a as a key node regulating cell survival pathways. Subsequent in vitro inhibition of miR-20a in neural stem cells preserved cell viability and reduced apoptosis under peroxide-induced oxidative stress.

The raw sequencing and proteomics datasets are deposited in public repositories (GEO and PRIDE) as detailed in the publication.
